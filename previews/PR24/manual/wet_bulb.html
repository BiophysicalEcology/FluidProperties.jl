Wet bulb temperature ​

The wet bulb temperature is the temperature read from a thermometer with its bulb covered with a wet wick and ventilated. It is a function of air temperature, humidity and pressure, and is calculated by wet_bulb_temperature. The relationship of wet bulb temperature to dry bulb temperature, vapor density, and relative humidity is shown in the psychometric diagram in Figure 15.

julia
using CairoMakie, FluidProperties, Unitful

wet_bulb_temperature(30.0u"°C", 0.5, 101325.0u"Pa")
295.3184936124306 K

The wet bulb temperature is returned in kelvin. Air temperature and pressure can be given in any unit, and relative humidity is a fraction between 0 and 1. A humidity outside of this range throws a DomainError.

julia
uconvert(u"°C", wet_bulb_temperature(86.0u"°F", 0.5, 1013.25u"hPa"))
22.16849361243061 °C

Methods ​

The formulation is chosen by passing a WetBulbMethod as the first argument. The default is DaviesJones.

MethodBasis
DaviesJonesPseudoadiabatic wet bulb, Davies-Jones (2008); accepts SpecificHumidity
StullEmpirical fit, Stull (2011); ignores pressure
BarenbrugPsychrometric equation, Barenbrug (1947)
SmithsonianPsychrometric equation, List (1971)
EnergyBalanceAdiabatic saturation energy balance, Zhang et al. (2021)
julia
T = 30.0u"°C"
RH = 0.5
P = 101325.0u"Pa"

methods = (DaviesJones(), Stull(), Barenbrug(), Smithsonian(), EnergyBalance())
[nameof(typeof(m)) => uconvert(u"°C", wet_bulb_temperature(m, T, RH, P)) for m in methods]
5-element Vector{Pair{Symbol, Quantity{Float64, 𝚯, Unitful.FreeUnits{(K,), 𝚯, Unitful.Affine{-5463//20}}}}}:
   :DaviesJones => 22.16849361243061 °C
         :Stull => 22.29683396268024 °C
     :Barenbrug => 22.003829445343968 °C
   :Smithsonian => 22.117686690545895 °C
 :EnergyBalance => 22.060765549225493 °C

The psychrometric methods, Barenbrug, Smithsonian and EnergyBalance, solve an equation for the wet bulb temperature and take a vapour_pressure_equation (GoffGratch by default) and the solver settings tolerance and max_iterations. With the Bolton vapour pressure equation, Smithsonian is the equation used in the NOAA wet bulb calculator (Lemke and Kjellstrom 2012).

julia
wet_bulb_temperature(Smithsonian(; vapour_pressure_equation = Bolton()), T, RH, P)
295.27397353982184 K

Comparison of the methods ​

julia
tairs = collect(0:2:50) .* u"°C" # sequence of air temperatures
twb(method, rh = 0.5, p = 101325.0u"Pa") = uconvert.(u"°C", wet_bulb_temperature.(method, tairs, rh, p))

fig, ax = figure_axis("Air temperature (°C)", "Wet bulb temperature (°C)")
for method in methods
    lines!(ax, ustrip.(tairs), ustrip.(twb(method)); linewidth = 2, label = string(nameof(typeof(method))))
end
axislegend(ax; position = :lt)
fig

The methods differ from DaviesJones by less than about 1 K at 50 °C and 50 % relative humidity, and by less at lower temperatures.

julia
fig, ax = figure_axis("Air temperature (°C)", "Difference from Davies-Jones (K)")
reference = twb(DaviesJones())
for method in methods[2:end]
    lines!(ax, ustrip.(tairs), ustrip.(u"K", twb(method) .- reference); linewidth = 2, label = string(nameof(typeof(method))))
end
axislegend(ax; position = :lb)
fig

Relative humidity and pressure ​

The wet bulb temperature rises with relative humidity and is the air temperature at saturation. At lower pressure it is lower, as there is less air to be cooled by evaporation.

julia
fig, ax = figure_axis("Air temperature (°C)", "Wet bulb temperature (°C)")
for rh in (0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0)
    lines!(ax, ustrip.(tairs), ustrip.(twb(DaviesJones(), rh)); linewidth = 2, label = "$(round(Int, 100rh)) % rh")
end
axislegend(ax; position = :lt)
fig

julia
fig, ax = figure_axis("Air temperature (°C)", "Wet bulb temperature (°C)")
for p in (101325.0u"Pa", 85000.0u"Pa", 70000.0u"Pa")
    lines!(ax, ustrip.(tairs), ustrip.(twb(DaviesJones(), 0.5, p)); linewidth = 2, label = "$p")
end
axislegend(ax; position = :lt)
fig

Davies-Jones method ​

wet_bulb_properties returns the equivalent temperature and equivalent potential temperature as well as the wet bulb temperature, as a WetBulbProperties. The lifting condensation temperature (Bolton 1980, eqn 22), the moist potential temperature (eqn 24) and the equivalent potential temperature (eqn 39) are found first, then the wet bulb temperature by Newton-Raphson iteration from the first guess of Davies-Jones (2008). The wet bulb and equivalent temperatures are NaN where the equivalent temperature is outside 200-600 K.

julia
wet_bulb_properties(30.0u"°C", 0.5, 101325.0u"Pa")
WetBulbProperties
  wet_bulb_temperature             = 295.318 K
  equivalent_temperature           = 343.73 K
  equivalent_potential_temperature = 342.441 K

The humidity can also be the specific humidity, which is treated as a mixing ratio:

julia
wet_bulb_properties(30.0u"°C", SpecificHumidity(0.0135), 101325.0u"Pa").wet_bulb_temperature
295.2483811604639 K

The iteration is chosen with the convergence keyword: RefinedNewton (the default) iterates to a tolerance and FixedNewton does at most four iterations.

julia
wet_bulb_temperature(DaviesJones(; convergence = FixedNewton()), T, RH, P) - wet_bulb_temperature(T, RH, P)
0.0 K

Natural wet bulb temperature ​

The natural wet bulb temperature is the temperature of a wetted thermometer bulb in the natural environment of wind and sun. natural_wet_bulb_temperature calculates it from the psychrometric wet bulb temperature and the wind speed (Bernard and Pourmoghani 1999). If the globe temperature is more than 4 K above the air temperature the effect of radiant heat is included. The air temperature is the default globe temperature.

julia
tw = wet_bulb_temperature(30.0u"°C", 0.5, 101325.0u"Pa")
natural_wet_bulb_temperature(30.0u"°C", tw, 1.0u"m/s")
295.63175386793336 K
julia
winds = (0.01:0.01:4.0) .* u"m/s" # sequence of wind speeds

fig, ax = figure_axis("Wind speed (m s⁻¹)", "Natural wet bulb temperature (°C)")
lines!(ax, ustrip.(winds), ustrip.(uconvert.(u"°C", natural_wet_bulb_temperature.(30.0u"°C", tw, winds))); linewidth = 2, label = "no radiant heat")
lines!(ax, ustrip.(winds), ustrip.(uconvert.(u"°C", natural_wet_bulb_temperature.(30.0u"°C", tw, winds; globe_temperature = 40.0u"°C"))); linewidth = 2, label = "globe 10 K above air")
hlines!(ax, ustrip(uconvert(u"°C", tw)); linestyle = :dash, color = :grey, label = "psychrometric")
axislegend(ax; position = :rt)
fig
